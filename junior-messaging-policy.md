# Junior two-way messaging policy

**Kinetic Hustl — Karl Hoschke**
**Current as at:** 12 November 2026
**Applies to:** every client under 18

Internal reference. The client-facing wording lives in the T&C form (section 4) and the
Junior Participation Agreement. If this file and those disagree, fix both.

---

## The rule

Two-way messaging in the app is **off by default** for every client under 18. A parent or
guardian can turn it on for their athlete. Nobody else can, including the athlete.

This replaces the previous blanket rule, which was one-way messaging for all juniors with no
way to change it.

## What the channel is for

- Questions about their program or a particular exercise
- Telling Karl how a session felt, or how they pulled up the next day
- Form check videos
- Session logistics: running late, needing to move a session

## What it is not for

- Anything that is not about training
- Anything urgent. Karl does not monitor the app outside working hours and may not see a
  message for a day or more. Urgent things go to the guardian, who contacts Karl directly.
- Anything the athlete would not want their parent or guardian to read

## The guardrail

The thread is between Karl and that athlete. Each junior has their own app account, and a
parent cannot see the thread inside the app.

**Any parent or guardian may ask Karl at any time for the record of what has been exchanged
with their athlete, and he will provide it.** No reason needed and no notice required. This is
a written term the guardian has already agreed to, not a favour granted on request.

The athlete is told this before the channel is switched on, in their own agreement, in their
own words. An athlete must never be able to believe the channel is private and find out later
that it is not.

## Out-of-scope messages

If an athlete raises something that is not about training, Karl replies once, briefly, saying
it is better raised with their parent or in person, and does not continue it in the channel.
He tells the guardian the same day.

**Exception: disclosure of harm.** If an athlete says something indicating they may be at risk
of harm, this policy stops governing and Karl follows his child-safety obligations. Where the
concern involves the guardian, it is not routed to the guardian.

> **Open.** That exception needs its own written procedure and is not one a messaging policy
> should try to carry. Karl holds a current Queensland Blue Card. Worth getting the procedure
> written properly, and worth asking whoever reviews the 12-week term to look at it.

## Other channels

The app is the only channel. If an athlete contacts Karl anywhere else — personal phone,
Instagram, email — he does not hold the conversation there. He replies once asking them to use
the app, or tells the guardian, and leaves it.

This applies whether or not two-way is switched on, and it is the reason the opt-in exists at
all: a closed channel does not stop a 14-year-old asking a question, it just moves the question
somewhere with no record.

## Turning it off

A guardian can switch it off at any time, in writing, with no reason given. Karl confirms in
writing once it is done.

Karl may also switch it off where the channel is being used outside its scope, having told the
guardian why.

## Turning 18

On the client's eighteenth birthday the junior terms stop applying. The channel carries on as
an ordinary adult client channel.

The guardian's right to request the record ends at that point. It covers the period while the
client was under 18 and does not extend to anything sent afterwards.

## Scope of the opt-in

**Per athlete, not per family.** Each junior has their own app account, their own agreement and
their own acknowledgement. A guardian may reasonably want it on for a 16-year-old and off for a
13-year-old, and the paperwork should let them.

**One rule for every age, 12 to 17.** No age tiers. The thing that varies is the guardian's
judgement about their own child, and the opt-in already captures that. Age tiers would add a
birthday problem and admin for no gain in safety.

## The record

Karl keeps the record for as long as he keeps the client's other records.

> **To verify.** The guardrail promises a record exists and persists. That promise is only as
> good as what the app actually retains. Confirm with Trainerize how long message history is
> kept, and whether it survives archiving or deleting an athlete's account. If it is not
> indefinite, export each junior thread quarterly and on exit, and keep the export with the
> client's file. Until that is confirmed, do not describe it to a parent as a complete
> permanent record.

## Where it is captured

| Step | Where | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Default stated | T&C form, section 4 | Guardian reads |
| 2. Opt-in chosen | T&C form, section 4 | Guardian chooses, on or off |
| 3. Athlete acknowledgement | Junior Participation Agreement, in person | Athlete, with Karl present |
| 4. Channel switched on | Trainerize | Karl, only after steps 2 and 3 |

Step 3 is deliberately not a tick-box in the guardian's form. The guardian fills that form in,
so a box there would be the guardian attesting on the athlete's behalf, which is precisely the
gap that lets an athlete believe the channel is private. The athlete's acknowledgement happens
in the agreement they sign themselves, with Karl in the room to check it landed.

**The channel does not get switched on until both exist.**

## Explaining it to a parent

> Each of the kids has their own account in the app, so if you turn messaging on, the thread is
> between me and them. You would not see it in the app yourself.
>
> So the deal is this: you can ask me at any time for everything that has been said, and I will
> send it to you. You do not need a reason and you do not need to ask in advance. That is
> written into the agreement rather than being something I offer if you ask.
>
> They get told that too, in their own agreement, in their own words. I would rather they know
> from the start that you can read it than find out later and feel caught out.
>
> It is for training: how a session felt, a question about an exercise, letting me know they are
> running late. If they bring up something that is not training, I will point them back to you
> and let you know the same day.
>
> You can turn it off whenever you like, just tell me.
